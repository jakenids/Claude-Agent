## Executive summary

Three to six bullets. Each states a *finding*, not a topic — "X outperforms Y
below 10k rows, and the gap reverses above that" rather than "performance was
examined." A reader who stops here should still be able to act.

## <Section 1>

Findings, with sources inline as `[label](url)`. Lead with the claim, then the
evidence. Where sources disagree, present both positions and say which has the
stronger support and why — or say the question is genuinely open.

Mark inference explicitly: *(inference — not stated in the sources)*.

## <Section 2..n>

Same shape, one section per outline heading from the approved plan.

## Open questions and gaps

- What the research could not settle, and why — thin sources, paywalled work,
  contradictory data, or a question no one appears to have asked.
- Claims found but not verifiable. Say where they came from.
- What would resolve each gap, if it's resolvable.

## Limitations

How to discount this report: source quality, recency, where vendor or advocacy
material dominated the available writing, and any part of the scope that got
lighter treatment than the rest.

## Sources

Numbered list. For each: title, publisher, date, URL, and a few words on what
it contributed. Sources that were consulted and rejected as unreliable go here
too, marked as such — knowing what was discarded is part of the record.
